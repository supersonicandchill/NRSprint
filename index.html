⚡ Ultra High-Yield Cram Sheet

RULE #1: Airway > Breathing > Circulation
Most NREMT questions are solved by identifying what kills first.
Blue + Tired = Ventilate
If respirations are inadequate or slow, use BVM immediately. Do NOT give high-flow O₂ to someone who isn't moving air.
Cool Clammy = Shocky
Early shock = tachycardia, pale, cool, anxious. Treat with oxygen, position, fluids (if ALS/transport available).
Kids Crash from Respiratory First
Children decompensate rapidly from airway/breathing problems. Adults from circulation.

ABC Priorities by Patient Type

Trauma: Airway first (with C-spine). Control bleeding. Rapid transport.
Medical: Airway, then O₂. Get history. Identify and treat reversible causes.
Respiratory Distress: High-flow O₂ first. Sit up. Listen. Transport.
Chest Pain: Oxygen, aspirin (if alert), position of comfort. Monitor. Rapid transport.

Red Flag Findings = Rapid Transport

📍 Tension pneumothorax (absent BS + JVD + hypotension)
📍 Flail chest with respiratory compromise
📍 Open pneumothorax (seal 3 sides)
📍 Uncontrolled bleeding
📍 Shock (hypotension + altered mental status)
📍 Altered LOC (stroke, head injury, intoxication)

Airway Devices Quick Reference

OPA (Oropharyngeal): Unconscious + no gag reflex. Measure angle of jaw to teeth.
NPA (Nasopharyngeal): Gag reflex present. Avoid with basilar skull fracture (CSF).
BVM: Slow/shallow breathing. Seal + ventilate at 500–600 mL, 10–12/min (adults). Be aggressive.
Suction: Tonsillar catheter for pooled secretions. Yankauer for suctioning mouth.

Stroke Recognition (FAST)

Face drooping | Arm drifting | Speech slurred | Time to EMS
Key: Last known well time = reperfusion window. Note the time.

Seizure Management

During: Protect from injury. Do NOT restrain. Turn head to side if possible. Time the seizure.
After: Recovery position. Oxygen. Reassure. Transport. Do NOT give anything by mouth.
Recurrent (status epilepticus): High-flow O₂. Rapid transport. Call ALS early.

Chest Pain Rule-Out

💊 Aspirin (if alert, no allergy, not asthmatic)
🫁 High-flow O₂
💨 Position of comfort (usually sitting)
📋 Get EKG (ALS)
🚗 Transport to facility with cath lab if STEMI suspected

Trauma Golden Hour Priorities

Scene Safety → BSI → Primary Survey (ABCs) → Secondary Survey (rapid head-to-toe)
C-spine: Immobilize if mechanism suggests injury (MOI). Maintain in-line stabilization.
Rapid Extrication: If trapped, use minimal handling. Spine precautions override comfort.
Load & Go: Critical trauma = on-scene time ≤10 min. Transport first, treat en route.

💊 Drugs & Doses (EMT-B Scope)

Medications EMT-B Can Administer

Oxygen (O₂)
Dose: Variable (10–15 L/min non-rebreather for hypoxia)
Use: Hypoxia, respiratory distress, chest pain, suspected shock
Aspirin
Dose: 325 mg (2 tablets of 81 mg, or 1 × 325 mg, depending on state)
Indications: Chest pain, alert, no allergy, not asthmatic
Contraindications: Allergy, asthma, GI bleed history
Oral Glucose
Dose: 15 g (1 tube, e.g., Insta-Glucose)
Use: Hypoglycemia (altered LOC, alert enough to swallow, blood glucose <70 if available)
RULE: Alert patient only. If no improvement in 10 min, transport.
Activated Charcoal (per state protocol)
Dose: 25–50 g PO
Use: Recent ingestion (alert, gag reflex)
RULE: Only if poison control or ALS directs.
Epinephrine Auto-Injector (if carrier or bystander supplies)
Dose: 0.3 mg IM (adult), 0.15 mg IM (child <25 kg)
Use: Anaphylaxis (wheezing, hives, facial swelling, hypotension)
RULE: Thigh IM. Massage site 10 sec. Repeat every 5–15 min if no improvement.
Nitroglycerin (if carrier has prescribed supply)
Dose: 0.3–0.6 mg sublingual
Use: Chest pain (alert, systolic BP > 90, HR < 100, approved per protocol)
Precautions: Do NOT give if on Viagra/similar (severe hypotension).

Medications ALS May Administer (Know for Test)

Epinephrine IV (Cardiac Arrest)
Dose: 1 mg IV push every 3–5 min
Amiodarone (VF/Pulseless VT)
Dose: 300 mg IV, then 150 mg
Sodium Bicarbonate (Severe Metabolic Acidosis)
Dose: 1 mEq/kg IV
Atropine (Bradycardia)
Dose: 0.5–1 mg IV, repeat every 3–5 min (max 3 mg)
